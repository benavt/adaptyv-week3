# Human GLP1R designs: the final 20

Final computational report | 25 October 2026

This report presents 20 protein designs selected for experimental evaluation against human GLP1R. Six are original LigandForge designs. Fourteen combine two previously designed binding domains with a new linker generated by AP Novo. The report gives every complete sequence, its predicted properties, how it was created, and the inputs and programs used to evaluate it.

All 20 passed two computational thresholds: an interface-confidence score, ipSAE, above 0.895 and a DeltaForge predicted binding affinity, Kd, below 10 nM. Candidates were then ranked by ipSAE, with the highest 20 retained. These predictions help prioritize experiments. Binding and receptor activation have not yet been demonstrated experimentally.

## 1  Design strategy and selection

GLP1R is the human glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor. The goal is to identify proteins that bind the receptor and could be tested for receptor activation. The fusion designs connect a domain aimed at the receptor's extracellular domain (ECD) to an original LigandForge design. This creates a hypothesis of coordinated engagement at two receptor regions; the calculations do not demonstrate that both domains engage as intended or that the receptor is activated.

The original designs were predicted against a 409-residue receptor construct. The fusion designs used a 440-residue construct. These different inputs matter when comparing the two groups. Their shared ranking follows the chosen computational selection rule; it is not a controlled experimental comparison.

### 1.1  Designs evaluated

| Stage | Completed work | How it contributed to the final selection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Original LigandForge designs | 1,000 sequences and 1,000 Boltz2 Exact complex predictions | Supplied original candidates and one parent domain for fusions |
| Active fusion campaign | 183 parent pairs, ten linker attempts each: 1,830 generated fusions and 1,830 Exact predictions | Supplied fusion candidates; reused pilot predictions were counted once |
| Extracellular-position screen | 1,725 fusion models retained; 105 set aside | Removed models whose free C terminus failed the extracellular-position check |

Two prohibited parent pairs, P000 and P001, were excluded from the active fusion campaign because their selected free C termini were on the intracellular side. The 1,830 active predictions include 20 reused pilot predictions and 1,810 later predictions. Other design and receptor-sampling work occurred during the project, but the counts above describe the branches contributing to this selection, rather than every project attempt.

### 1.2  Ranking rule

The strict thresholds were ipSAE greater than 0.895 and predicted Kd less than 10 nM. Among qualifying designs, higher ipSAE determined final rank. The predefined tie-breakers were lower predicted Kd, then design group and design name; no tie affected the cutoff. There was no diversity-based reordering or fixed quota for either group. A candidate's earlier rank within its own group is kept separate from its final rank.

## 2  Programs, inputs and evaluation methods

### 2.1  Receptor sequences and target regions

The target was human GLP1R, UniProt P43220-1. The challenge reference structure was PDB 6X18, author chain R. Original-design complex predictions used wild-type receptor residues 24-432, totaling 409 amino acids. ECD-parent and fusion predictions used residues 24-463, totaling 440 amino acids. Both complete receptor sequences are printed in section 7. In the final complex models, the candidate is chain A and the receptor is chain B. Receptor position B1 corresponds to UniProt position 24; add 23 to convert any native receptor position to its UniProt number.

The original LigandForge request specified 27 receptor-pocket positions: 141, 144, 145, 148, 152, 190, 194, 197, 201, 230, 233, 234, 237, 241, 298, 299, 300, 306, 309, 310, 313, 372, 380, 384, 387, 388 and 391, in the submitted structure's numbering. Pocket expansion was set to zero. The provider's input-preparation software instead reported fourteen positions, 60, 61, 76-80 and 105-111, in a different numbering system. Its receptor structure differed from the submitted structure. The available records do not establish the correspondence between these lists, so the requested pocket cannot be assumed to describe the actual generation input precisely.

The ECD-parent route used an open-receptor computational model identified as seed 156, sample 1. It targeted Arg40, which is receptor position B17. Gln37 (B14) and Glu34 (B11) were additional monitored positions. Only the Arg40 contact was required in the downstream contact screen; the other two were optional.

### 2.2  LigandForge: original sequence generation

LigandForge version 6.5.21 generated the original population. Its input-preparation software was version 6.5.13. The saved configuration requested a binding segment of 20-60 amino acids, followed by the fixed six-residue sequence GSGSGS and four terminal residues. Complete exported designs were 32-70 amino acids long. A separate provider summary describes a 20-40-residue binding segment; the reason for this discrepancy is unknown. Each selected sequence is printed in full, so its actual composition is unambiguous.

Generation used cell-free-expression guidance and recombinant-only residues. Quality-guided sampling and extracellular-domain trimming were disabled. No charge restriction, paralog target or pH condition was specified. The selected original designs retain their complete exported sequences, including the GSGSGS region and terminal residues. No mutation, shortening or additional cyclization chemistry was introduced during report preparation.

Original generated complex structures were not retained. Consequently, the later Boltz2 Exact structures cannot be compared with an original LigandForge structural reference. The provider also exported creation timestamps later than a recorded folding-completion timestamp. Their literal order is inconsistent and is not used to infer the sequence of execution.

### 2.3  AP Novo: extracellular-domain parent generation

All ECD parents represented in the final 20 were generated by AP Novo. The input was the complete 440-residue open-receptor model, with no starting binder, GLP1 peptide, explicit membrane or glycan atoms. Generation used 1,000 sampling steps. Selected parent conditions requested new binders of either 20-40 or 40-60 amino acids.

The receptor sequence was fixed throughout. Some conditions also fixed all receptor coordinates. Other conditions allowed receptor positions B106-116 to move, while keeping their sequence fixed. This flexible receptor segment is different from the new linker later placed between the two binder domains. Before generation, CPU checks confirmed which atoms could move and which sequences had to remain fixed. Each candidate's history states its parent condition.

Boltz2 Fast provided an initial screen. The top 100 designs by Fast-ipSAE from each of twelve conditions were taken forward for standalone Boltz2 Exact prediction. Parent ranks and scores in the candidate histories refer to these standalone predictions. A high-scoring fusion can contain a parent that scored poorly alone; the fusion result does not validate that parent's standalone pose.

### 2.4  AP Novo: joining the two parent domains

Each fusion retains an ECD parent and a LigandForge parent intact, connected in the stated order. The two parent structures were positioned using their receptor context. Pair selection preferred an exposed free C terminus on the extracellular side, then the shorter gap between the first domain's C terminus and the second domain's N terminus. The exposure criterion required a terminal exposure score of at least 0.25 and an extracellular height above 19.7 Å in the oriented membrane reference.

The minimum requested linker length was the larger of eight residues or the junction gap divided by 2.8 Å, rounded upward, minus one residue. The maximum was twelve residues longer. Ten explicit random seeds were used per active pair. AP Novo generated the linker while holding both parent sequences and all retained parent atoms fixed. Each candidate history gives the actual linker sequence and length, allowed length range, orientation, seed and attempt number.

To fit the generator's input limit, the receptor context contained the complete ECD, native receptor positions 1-105, plus nearby receptor fragments. All contacts within 4.5 Å were mandatory. Additional context was selected within 10 Å with a one-residue flank; that radius could be reduced to keep the total input within 512 residues. Each candidate's input record below lists the exact retained receptor intervals and actual radius. Their sequences can be read directly from the 440-residue receptor sequence in section 7. Full 440-residue receptor context was restored for the subsequent Exact prediction.

### 2.5  Boltz2 Exact: predicting candidate-receptor complexes

Every reported final model was produced by Boltz2 Exact using the complete candidate sequence and the appropriate receptor sequence. Settings were random seed 101, three recycling steps, 200 sampling steps and one diffusion sample. Up to 8,192 sequences were allowed in the multiple-sequence alignment (MSA). The binder had no alignment beyond its own sequence. No structural templates or online MSA server were used. Target alignments and native input features were generated or validated on CPU before GPU prediction. The original target-alignment preparation used ColabFold 1.5.2 with UniRef30 version 2202 and no environmental-sequence search.

The principal ranking score, ipSAE, was calculated with the DunbrackLab implementation using a predicted alignment error cutoff of 10 Å and a distance cutoff of 15 Å. The larger of the two directional chain scores was used for selection. The individual directional scores remain visible for fusion designs. Interface confidence does not measure binding affinity or receptor activation.

The report also includes ipTM and, where available, pDockQ, pDockQ2 and the local interaction score (LIS). These summarize different aspects of the predicted interface and should not be treated as interchangeable probabilities. Local structural confidence, pLDDT, is given for the full fusion and its separate domains and linker. Fusion segment values use a 0-100 scale; the original population's saved complex mean uses a 0-1 scale and is labeled accordingly.

### 2.6  Structural consistency and extracellular-position checks

DockQ and TM-score compared fusion Exact structures with their computational design references. DockQ summarizes agreement in contacts and binding pose. TM-score summarizes structural similarity. Neither comparison uses an experimentally determined structure. A low value can therefore reveal that the predicted fusion differs substantially from its intended design even when its interface-confidence score is high. Root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) measures positional differences in Å; the receptor-core RMSD uses a fit to the receptor core, rather than an independent fit to the binder.

The membrane-position screen used the orientation of 6X18 in the Orientations of Proteins in Membranes (OPM) reference. A membrane half-thickness of 14.9 Å, a 3.0 Å outward margin and a 1.8 Å atomic-radius allowance gave the 19.7 Å extracellular threshold. Alignment used 253 receptor-core C-alpha atoms from native positions 117-234, 240-315, 338-346 and 351-400. The final selected fusions passed the post-prediction free-C-terminal height check. This does not establish that an attached assay tag would be accessible.

Additional screening settings included a 2.0 Å minimum binder-receptor heavy-atom distance, a 4.5 Å required Arg40 side-chain contact, and 15 Å exclusion zones around the ND2 atoms of potential glycosylation sites at UniProt positions 63, 82 and 115. These are geometric screens. Glycan occupancy was not established, and explicit glycans and membrane atoms were not simulated.

### 2.7  DeltaForge: predicting binding affinity

DeltaForge evaluated each complete candidate as one chain against one receptor chain from the saved Boltz2 Exact model. The intact fusion included both parent domains and the full linker. The original candidates retained the 409-residue receptor; fusions retained the 440-residue receptor. Structures were supplied in PDB format, and sequence and model correspondence were checked before joining the affinity results to the confidence scores.

All selected results used the DeltaForge v10.2 unified parallel scorer, dated 8 May 2026, with the Boltz2-calibrated gradient-boosted affinity model dated 29 March 2026. Kd is reported in nanomolar (nM); lower values indicate stronger predicted binding. No candidate-specific uncertainty interval was returned. Six selected fusions exceed 140 residues, placing them in the provider's longer-sequence category; this label does not establish how well the affinity model is calibrated for them.

The requests did not include the folding-confidence values needed for the provider's combined binder/non-binder classification, so that classification was left unassigned. The separately calculated ipSAE values do not change the saved provider response. The returned energy field had no explicit unit and was not used to recalculate Kd. Contact, hydrogen-bond, salt-bridge and hydrophobic-contact counts in the candidate tables are provider-defined geometric features, rather than experimentally verified interactions.

### 2.8  Reading the candidate records

Each candidate record identifies its complete sequence, parents when applicable, generation inputs, prediction settings and results. Parent names, random seeds, residue intervals and program versions are retained because they explain how the design was produced. Sequence and structural-input checks confirmed that each selected affinity result belonged to the intended complete molecule. This revision uses saved calculations; no new design, folding or affinity predictions were run.

### 7.3  How the designs progressed through the workflow

The original population was generated with LigandForge and evaluated with Boltz2 Exact. Its generation settings were documented on 16 October. Exported creation timestamps conflict with an earlier recorded folding completion, so the precise timestamp order remains unresolved.

The ECD-parent campaign was configured on 19 October. Saved AP Novo outputs were recovered during the 21 October processing repair, then screened with Boltz2 Fast and taken forward for the 23 October standalone Exact evaluation. Recovery and transport steps did not create new scientific designs.

On 24 October, the selected ECD and LigandForge parents were paired, and AP Novo generated intervening linkers. Removing the two prohibited pairs left 183 active pairs and 1,830 candidate fusions. Complete candidates were predicted again with the full 440-residue receptor using Boltz2 Exact. The 25 October ranking combined the saved interface scores with DeltaForge results for the two scored groups of 50. The final filter and ranking produced the 20 sequences in this report.
